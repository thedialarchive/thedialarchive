Orient Kamasu Review: Best Budget Dive Watch?

The Orient Kamasu is the dive watch that made the watch community forget about the discontinued Seiko SKX. For, you get a sapphire crystal, 200m water resistance, an in-house automatic movement, and build quality that rivals watches costing twice as much. After six months of daily wear, here's whether it lives up to the hype.

Specifications

First Impressions

When you unbox the Kamasu, the first thing that hits you is the crystal. It's sapphire, which is insane at this price point. Most watches under $300 use mineral crystal or hardlex. The Kamasu gives you scratch-resistant sapphire, and it's beautifully domed for that vintage dive watch aesthetic.

The second thing you notice is the weight. This feels substantial. The case finishing is excellent, the bracelet is solid, and the overall build quality screams "I cost more than $225." Orient nailed the value proposition here.

The Movement: F6922

Orient's in-house F6922 movement is a workhorse. It's hand-windable (you can manually wind it with the crown), it hacks (seconds hand stops when you pull the crown for precise time-setting), and it's been proven reliable across thousands of Orient watches.

The accuracy has been solid for me, running about +10 seconds per day. Not chronometer-certified, but perfectly acceptable for a sub-$300 automatic. The 40-hour power reserve means you can take it off Friday night and it'll still be running Monday morning.

Through the display caseback, you can see the movement. It's not decorated (no fancy Geneva stripes or perlage), but it's clean and finished well for this price point.

Build Quality and Design

The Kamasu follows classic dive watch design language. Rotating bezel, applied indices, cathedral hands, date window at 3 o'clock. It's not trying to reinvent anything; it's executing the basics excellently.

The bezel action is firm and precise. The crown screws down smoothly. The case has nice brushing on top, polished on the sides. The lume is strong – you can actually read the time in the dark, which is the whole point of a dive watch.

At 42mm diameter and 46mm lug-to-lug, it wears comfortably on most wrists. On my 7-inch wrist, it's perfect. If you have a 6.5-inch wrist, it'll still work. At 13mm thick, it's chunky but not ridiculous.

The Green Dial (or Blue, or Red, or Black)

The Kamasu comes in multiple dial colors. The green is probably the most popular – it's got that Rolex Submariner "Hulk" vibe without the Rolex price tag. The sunburst finish catches light beautifully.

The black dial is the most versatile. The blue is classic. The red is bold. Whichever you choose, the finishing is excellent for the price.

Daily Wear Experience

This is where the Kamasu really shines. It's comfortable enough to wear all day, rugged enough to not worry about, and versatile enough to work in most situations. I've worn it with suits, with t-shirts, swimming, hiking – it just works.

The bracelet is solid and doesn't rattle. The clasp is well-made. You can swap in a NATO strap or rubber strap easily if you prefer. The watch wears lighter than it looks, which is a good thing for all-day comfort.

The 200m water resistance means you can actually use this as intended. Swimming, snorkeling, even diving if you're so inclined. This is a real tool watch, not just a dive watch in aesthetics only.

What Works

  • Sapphire crystal at this price is incredible
  • In-house automatic movement with hand-winding
  • 200m water resistance with screw-down crown
  • Excellent build quality and finishing
  • Strong, usable lume
  • Solid bracelet with good clasp
  • Multiple dial color options
  • Wears well on most wrists

What Doesn't

  • Relatively short 40-hour power reserve
  • 13mm thickness won't slip under tight shirt cuffs
  • No micro-adjustments on bracelet clasp
  • Movement finishing is basic (but this is nitpicking)

How It Compares

vs Seiko 5 Sports: The Kamasu has sapphire crystal and 200m water resistance vs the Seiko's hardlex and 100m. The Kamasu wins on paper, though the Seiko offers more color variety.

vs Orient Mako II: The Kamasu is the refined version of the Mako. Better finishing, cleaner dial, improved bracelet. Worth premium.

vs Casio Duro: The Duro is $60 and quartz. The Kamasu is $225 and automatic with sapphire. Different value propositions entirely.

The Verdict

The Orient Kamasu is the best value in dive watches right now. Sapphire crystal, 200m water resistance, in-house automatic movement, excellent build quality – all . This is the watch that replaced the Seiko SKX in the watch community's collective consciousness, and it deserves that position.

If you want a legitimate dive watch under $300, this should be at the top of your list. It punches way above its weight class in every category that matters.
