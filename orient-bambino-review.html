What Makes It Feel Expensive

The Bambino has this premium presence that catches people off guard when you tell them the price. Here's what Orient got right.

The domed mineral crystal is the first thing you notice. It catches light beautifully and gives the watch that vintage dress watch aesthetic. When light hits it at the right angle, the whole dial seems to glow. That alone makes it feel more expensive than it is.

The Dial and Hands

Orient makes the Bambino in about 50 different versions. Roman numerals, stick indices, different dial colors, different hand styles. I'm personally a fan of the green dial versions, but honestly, you can't go wrong with any of them.

The Movement

The F6724 automatic movement is hand-windable, which is rare at this price. You can wind it manually or just wear it. The rotor is visible through the exhibition caseback, and while it's not decorated like a Swiss movement, it's clean and well-finished for the money.

Where You Notice Price Tag

Let me be honest about where this watch shows its budget. If you're comparing it to A dress watch, here's what you'll notice.

The Rotor Noise

The automatic rotor is louder than more expensive watches. It's not obnoxious, but in a quiet room, you can hear it when you move your wrist. Some people don't care. Some people find it charming. If you're sensitive to watch noise, it might bother you.

The Thickness

At around 11.8mm thick, the Bambino is a little chunky for a dress watch. Ideally, a dress watch should slip under a shirt cuff easily. The Bambino does it, but it's not as sleek as thinner options.

That thickness comes from the domed crystal and the movement. It's a trade-off. You get that beautiful dome, but you lose some elegance in the profile.
